Case · The story the data tells

Six peoples · one shared will · a decade of operation

In the far northeast of the Colombian Amazon, where the Vichada River meets the marshlands and moriche palm groves, six Indigenous peoples — Sikuani, Piaroa, Puinave, Curripaco, Cubeo and Piapoco — autonomously administer one of the largest continuous forest blocks in the country. 16 former resguardos consolidated into a single Unified Indigenous Resguardo of 1,150,212 net hectares. Not a national park. Not a government concession to third parties. It is an Indigenous resguardo managed by its own authorities through ACATISEMA (Association of Indigenous Councils and Traditional Authorities of the Matavén Forest).

In 2016, ACATISEMA registered the territory with the Verra Carbon Standard as a REDD+ project under identifier 1566. A year later, they simultaneously validated under the Climate, Community & Biodiversity (CCB) standard, a dual certification requiring not only deforestation reduction but also tangible social benefit and demonstrable biodiversity conservation.

What matters is not the certification itself — many REDD+ projects have been questioned. What matters is who operates it: the chain of custody, decisions over issued credits, signed community agreements (COMMS_AGR_1566_28OCT2016) and verification reports for 2013-2015, 2016-2017 and 2018-2019 all flow through Indigenous governance. Without international intermediaries diluting political control.

Why this case matters for Brasília
When Panel 4 donors ask "which direct fiduciary mechanism works in practice?", the answer is not a hypothesis: it is Matavén. A project audited by international third parties, operated by a national Indigenous organization, over a decade, in Colombia.

Methodological transition · VM0007 → VM0048

The 2020-2022 vintage is certified under VM0048, the consolidated framework that replaces VM0007 and incorporates a jurisdictional baseline allocation. This transition directly responds to the voluntary standard update following the comparative baseline analysis published in 2021. The 2023+ period continues under the same updated methodological framework.
