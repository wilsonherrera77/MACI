Paragraph 01 · Sovereignty

Indigenous data sovereignty and knowledge systems

Indigenous data sovereignty is not a technical aspiration · it is an operational condition for peoples to safeguard the biocultural information of their territories under CARE principles (Collective benefit · Authority to control · Responsibility · Ethics). The Declaration of Sabios and Sabias COP16 (Centro Java Liviana · Muysca People · October 2024) states it clearly: "our knowledge systems, given from creation and transmitted from generation to generation, are fundamental to protecting Mother Earth". In Colombia, the National Indigenous Organisation (ONIC) and the Territorial Monitoring System (SMT) are building the MACI Framework, articulating 5 operational modules around a Data Council · FPIC protocols · Nagoya ABS · biobank with Indigenous chain of custody · and bioeconomy with fair benefit-sharing. The evidence already exists: the Useful Plants × Resguardos Catalogue 2024 catalogues 5,690 geolocated species with Kew Gardens and GBIF endorsement · and custodian peoples steward close to 18 million hectares in NON-Amazonian ecosystems (páramos · Chocó biogeographic · savannahs · drylands) in addition to Amazonian territories. The Brasília Declaration must recognise data sovereignty as a fundamental right complementary to the CBD's OS8j, and commit concrete mechanisms so national Indigenous organisations are recognised fiduciarily without intermediaries that dilute political control.

Paragraph 02 · Financing

Direct financing without intermediaries

Less than 1% of the USD 1.7 billion committed at Glasgow's Forest Tenure Pledge has reached national Indigenous organisations directly. This architecture is structural · two decades of climate funds channelled via international NGOs have produced fragmented capacity · short projects · and dependency. The Declaration of Sabios and Sabias COP16 states it literally: "to agree on direct financing strategies with Indigenous Peoples · enabling execution and administration to be carried out directly by Indigenous governance structures for the implementation of Life Plans". The TRÜA World Summit to CBD's OS8j reaffirms this. The Brasília Summit must move beyond declarations of intent toward concrete fiduciary mechanisms: (a) direct accreditations such as GCF DAE for national Indigenous organisations · (b) bilateral country packages with Indigenous title · (c) replicable national territorial funds (Podáali Brazil model) · and (d) philanthropic windows with minimum direct-Indigenous quotas. The commitment must be quantitative · traceable · with deadlines.

Paragraph 03 · Governance

Biocultural governance and returning to origin

Biocultural governance requires integrating three layers that today operate separately: traditional knowledge · formal science · and sustainable markets. The Declaration of Sabios and Sabias COP16 makes the underlying political-spiritual call: "to return to origin to change the ambitious, violent, selfish and irresponsible ways of existing" that devastate Mother Earth · and demands recognition of "vital spaces, sacred sites and natural elements · waters, mountains, rivers, páramos, seas, savannahs, forests and jungles · as living beings, subjects of rights". The MACI Framework translates this mandate into operational architecture: community biocultural inventories with QA/QC and mandatory results-back · genetic capacities and biobank with specific FPIC and Nagoya ABS agreements · bio-business incubation with fair benefit-sharing clauses. Geographic coverage spans Colombia's 5 macro-regions with verifiable cases: Matavén VCS+CCB certified · georeferenced Useful Plants Catalogue · U'wa SU-039/1997 ruling · Sierra Nevada's Línea Negra · Chocó Embera. The Brasília Declaration must recognise Indigenous bioeconomy as legitimate own-economy · not market niche · and commit stable multi-year financial support to comprehensive programmes like MACI · not isolated 18-month projects.
